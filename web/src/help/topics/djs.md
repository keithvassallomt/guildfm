A DJ talks between songs on a station: introducing what's next, sharing a bit of trivia, mentioning the weather, and giving shout-outs when requests play.

## Add a DJ

1. In the station's editor, turn on **Use a DJ**.
2. Under **Which DJ**, pick a DJ from another station, or **New DJ**.
3. For a new DJ, give them a **DJ name**, and describe their **Personality**: how they talk, their sense of humour, what they like to talk about.
4. Choose a **Voice**, and listen to it (below).
5. Choose **How often the DJ talks**, and **What the DJ says**.
6. Save the station.

![Choosing the DJ](shot:station-dj)

## One DJ, several stations

A DJ can host any number of stations. Changing their name, personality or voice in one station's editor changes them on all of them, and the editor says which other stations those are. How often they talk, and what they say, is up to each station.

## The voice

Voices are listed under **OpenAI**, and under **ElevenLabs** for voices made especially for GuildFM, such as Guildbert. Of OpenAI's, Marin and Cedar sound the most natural. ElevenLabs voices can act things out, laughing or sighing in the right places.

To hear one, pick a phrase, such as **Track intro**, or **Your own words** to type your own, and tap **Preview**. It plays on this device, not the office speakers, in the DJ's personality.

![The voice, and hearing it](shot:station-voice)

## How often, and what they say

- **How often the DJ talks**: every so many songs, or every so many minutes. They always talk on the first song after the station comes on.
- **What the DJ says**: **Just announce the songs** keeps it short. **Add commentary** adds trivia, the weather, and the odd story about the song.
- **Announce requests**: the DJ says who asked for a song, unless they've turned that off on their Profile. With it on, the DJ announces every request, however often they'd normally talk.

![How often the DJ talks](shot:station-talk)

The DJ always talks between songs, never over one, so every room hears it at the same time. A line that isn't ready in time is left out. Announcements always go first.
